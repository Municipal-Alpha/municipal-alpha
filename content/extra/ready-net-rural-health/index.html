Part 1

What is changing for 2027

Each state gets a new amount every year, and each state decides its own next rounds. Below: the federal clock, then what is on record about 2027 in each state and what the state has not published.

The federal clock and rules
  • By 31 October 2026 CMS sets each state's amount for fiscal year 2027. source
  • Budget year two starts on 31 October 2026. source
  • Each year CMS re-scores part of each state's score from the state's annual reporting. source
  • The rural part of the score is set once. source
  • A state receives more as it makes progress on its initiatives, measured against all other states. source
  • Between years a state may adjust its budget. source
  • Each year's money can be spent until the end of the following fiscal year. source
  • For 2026 money that is 30 September 2027. source
  • Money promised to a provider but not yet paid out does not count as spent. source
  • Only a state can apply for and be awarded this money. Cities, counties, hospitals and nonprofits cannot apply. source
  • A state may pass the money down, by subaward or contract, to local partners. source
  • Telehealth equipment is listed as a possible use of the money. source
  • Broadband infrastructure cannot be paid for with it. source

Kiosks and portals are not named in the federal notice or FAQ. They could fit its categories for consumer-facing technology, which the notice ties to chronic disease, and for IT hardware. That is our reading, not a quotation. CMS decides.

Mississippi in 2027

What is on record

  • Year-one telehealth money must be spent by 31 July 2027. source
  • The state's November 2025 plan calls telehealth equipment and connectivity grants one-time. source
  • That plan has the first providers connected by December 2027. source
  • New grant opportunities open in October 2026, for care gaps and medical-record systems. They are not telehealth rounds. source
  • In November 2025 the state said it would explore an advisory committee. We found no record that one exists. source

What the state has not published

  • A year-two amount, or an updated plan since the November 2025 application.
  • A second telehealth round.
  • Any public meeting on 2027.
Missouri in 2027

What is on record

  • Year-one money can be spent through 30 September 2027. source
  • One of the state's two federal scores is redone every year on its progress. source
  • A second funding phase, expected at nearly $40 million, spans years one and two. source
  • Its final amount still awaits federal approval. source
  • That phase was open to hospitals and other organizations that expand rural access. source
  • Its deadline was 28 August 2026. source
  • Each hub's leadership board sets the hub's priorities. source
  • The board decides together, and the anchor carries the decisions out. source
  • With its board, each anchor selects a limited number of local programs each year. source
  • The hospital association reports the telehealth request for information is for planning only. source

What the state has not published

  • What follows the telehealth request for information, and when.
  • A budget for years two to five.
  • Any public session after the 13 and 14 July 2026 roadshows, or whether hub boards meet in public.
Maine in 2027

What is on record

  • Maine has already filed its year-two plan. Approval is expected around the end of October 2026. source
  • The plan was filed on 28 August 2026 with minor changes. source
  • Maine expects to hear from CMS in mid-October, before the formal notice on 31 October. source
  • The state says the re-scoring leans heavily on work already done. source
  • Ideas outside this year's projects may help inform later years. source
  • The state takes no unsolicited proposals. source
  • The contractor says the state welcomes input on more solutions for later years. source
  • Year-one money must be spent by 30 September 2027. source
  • The contractor will explore a vendor registry for year two and beyond. source
  • The advisory committee meets on 7 October 2026. The public may listen. source
  • Two of the contractor's 16 rounds are open now, due 16 and 22 October 2026. The other 14 have no dates yet. source

What the state has not published

  • The year-two plan itself. The state has described it only as minor changes to year one.
  • A round that towns or libraries can apply to.
  • Dates for later rounds.

Mississippi

In Mississippi, providers applied straight to the state, with no middleman. An independent panel scored each application, the Health Department vetted it, and the Governor announced the awards. So the buyers are the named awardees: 24 of them won 27 awards under the telehealth hub, equipment and education program. Awards are paid back to providers after they spend.

$205,990,180 in year one
Held by
Run by the Governor's RHT Program Office; the Health Department handles applications. 167 awards announced so far.

We did not find the 27 telehealth applications published. The form asks for each project and its budget. Vendors were not to be named in it, though uploaded price quotes may name one.

Missouri

Missouri is the consortium shape Luke described: 27 hubs. Each hub's leadership board picks local programs, the anchor carries them out, and the regional network and the state office approve. Seven regional networks sit between the state and the hubs. Once a program is approved, the network contracts with partners and vendors on the hub's behalf, through one non-profit, Healthier Communities Together. Telehealth went out as a request for information that closed on 9 September 2026. The hospital association reports it is for planning only and will not result directly in an award.

$216M in year one
Held by
The state's Rural Health Transformation Office. About $52 million of year one goes to regional networks and hubs.
