What we actually do

We trace the full chain of events that leads up to a town switching payment vendors, or picking one for the first time, not just the moment right before it happens, back to the earliest point in the record a revenue team could actually act on it. Then we watch for that same early point showing up elsewhere, so the lead reaches you as early as the record allows, not after a competitor already has it. Skokie and Grafton, above, are both that early point showing up, not just documents we happened to find.

That comes from the public record of over 4,500 municipalities, all of it technically public, almost none of it searchable at the scale a revenue team would need to act on it consistently.

How this would actually work

A standing watch reads new public filings across your footprint every day, things like budget objectives that name a processor by name, board packets naming a competitor mid-decision, operational complaints tied to a specific vendor, and the account-level pattern that tends to precede either a renewal fight or a genuine switch. Each finding links back to the source document, dated, so it's a lead you can act on the same week, not a summary you have to take on faith.
