Maine towns are deciding where e-bikes belong

Nobody passed a statewide law. Instead, across 2023–2026, roughly ten Maine towns have been working it out one meeting at a time — a scooter contract here, a parks ordinance there, a select-board complaint about kids on North Union Road. All of it sits in public meeting records. Here is the thread, assembled from the towns' own documents.

Early look — unlisted, shared directly. Sources verified against each town's published record.

The one real contract

Old Town ran Bird scooters, then signed an e-bike share

April 2023 · City Council minutes
A councilor asks "if the Bird scooters will be coming back this spring" — Bird was operating in Old Town.
Min._April_18_2023_website.pdf, oldtown-me.gov
May 2024 · committee minutes
BACTS (the Bangor-area transportation system) partners with Tandem Mobility on a regional e-bike rental program: "Unlike the bird scooters, this program would have set locations that the bikes need to be dropped off." Motion to have the City Manager sign the MOU approved 4-0.
committee minutes, oldtown-me.gov
September 2024 · City Council minutes
The council accepts the Operating Agreement between Tandem Mobility and the City of Old Town and authorizes the City Manager to sign. "Approved all in favor, 6-0." The only operator contract in Maine's searchable municipal record.
Min._Reg._Council_Mtg._09.03.24.pdf, oldtown-me.gov
The 2025–2026 pattern

Parks ordinances are where the question gets settled

June 2, 2026 · Brewer City Council agenda · Order 2026-C012
A proposed amendment to the parks ordinance defines "electrically assisted vehicle" — "this term includes, but is not limited to, electric bicycles and electric scooters" — and would permit them "on multi-use paths, including the Riverwalk, unless otherwise posted." Agenda-sourced: proposed, not yet confirmed adopted.
brewermaine.gov →
June 2025 · Presque Isle parks conduct ordinance, Chapter 40
The ordinance now carries a codified definition: "'Electric Bicycle' is a two-wheeled, pedal-powered Class 1 vehicle… up to a maximum assisted speed of 20 mph" — and folds e-bikes into the meaning of "bicycle" throughout.
presqueislemaine.gov →
January 2026 · Bangor City Forest rules
"Motorized recreational activities are not allowed, but electric bikes are allowed at this time" — an explicit, provisional allowance on trails.
City Forest rules attachment, council materials, bangormaine.gov
The friction

Where it shows up as a complaint first

East Millinocket has been working the enforcement side since 2024: a resident's concern about "kids going through a stop sign on scooters" led the town administrator, two selectmen, and the Chief of Police to convene on a plan; by August 2025 the select board was still discussing "speeding, scooters, and safety risks" and ordinance amendment procedures. In Union, May 2026: a resident "encountered kids on bikes and an electric scooter down the middle of the road." This is how regulation usually starts — as minutes, months before it becomes an ordinance.

The programs

And where it shows up as planning

Yarmouth's Bike & Pedestrian Committee is running a 2026 E-Micromobility Safety & Education Campaign jointly with the police department and a UMaine Safe Routes to School capstone. And five 2025–2026 comprehensive or climate plans — South Portland, Falmouth, Cape Elizabeth, Bridgton, Bangor — name a bikeshare program as a strategy, with Portland granting development-review points for bike share.

How this was assembled — and what we ruled out

Every quote was verified against the full text of the town's own published document, not a search snippet. Dates are each document's own meeting date. The searchable slice covers 169 of the 191 Maine municipalities in our corpus (about 31% of collected Maine documents are full-text indexed), so absence here is not absence in the world — Portland's street-level scooter history, for instance, sits outside this slice.

Ruled out as noise, by reading the documents: Bar Harbor's "Pedego Electric Bikes" hits are property-tax bills for a retail shop; Farmington's electric scooter is a municipal surplus auction (current bid: $19); Cape Elizabeth's "scooters" are mobility aids; one Presque Isle hit is a resident surnamed Van Scooter. Lime, Spin, and Veo appear nowhere in searchable Maine records as operators.
