The catalog problem

You are building a town-wide catalog of programs, activities, and events across schools, parks and rec, camps, and libraries. Two of the hardest parts of that build are the same problem underneath: the town's own record of what it runs is scattered across a parks board here, a library trustee minutes there, a school-district calendar somewhere else, and a camp brochure posted on the rec department's own site.

It is all public, in the narrowest sense of the word: roughly ninety-nine percent of it never reaches a search engine. Catalog-by-hand becomes catalog-by-whoever-clicks-through-every-town's-website-every-week. That is what Municipal Alpha runs at scale, and it is the wedge in Schola's direction.

Two distinct MA offerings for Schola. Coverage across your four verticals, honest

Parks & rec
Strong. Parks and Recreation boards, department reports, recurring program calendars, capital and RFP items, all captured where the municipal publishing surface reaches them.
Libraries
Strong where the library board is municipal. Trustee minutes, event calendars, program flyers when posted in board packets.
Camps
Partial. Municipal rec-department summer camp brochures and registration flyers flow through parks-rec publishing. Independent day and specialty camps do not.
Schools*
Patchy. School districts are typically separate legal entities from the town, publishing on their own infrastructure. MA reaches district content where it appears in municipal-adjacent surfaces: joint capital plans, shared-facility agreements, town-council school-liaison reports. A systematic school-district crawl would need explicit scoping. * The four ScholaOne signal shapes listed in Layer 2 above are what a ScholaOne feed would look for once the district-level crawl is added, not shapes MA delivers systematically today.

Questions where your answers change how we calibrate

Genuine discussion topics, not a checklist. The first four route the calibration; the last two open the shape of the relationship past week six.

Which of your two products carries the Layer 2 lead-gen feed for the six-week calibration, ScholaOne, Assembly, or both in parallel? And within whichever, which of the four verticals (schools, parks & rec, camps, libraries) is the first wedge?
Determines whether we run one set of watch terms or two, whether the 30-minute check-in is one meeting or two, and whether to prioritize school-district crawl work (currently the weakest of the four coverage rows) for the ScholaOne side.
Who at Schola uses this data day-to-day, sales, you as founder, marketing, a community-manager role?
Sets the delivery format. A PDF for a founder-scan lands differently than a CSV a salesperson hits every morning, and the 30-minute check-in works only if the same person shows up.
What geographies and population bands is your ICP today, for each product if they differ?
Sizes MA's data reach against your actual market, and lets us cut the six-week target list against towns and districts the pipeline already covers well. Assembly's community-org footprint and ScholaOne's district footprint may not overlap cleanly.
What signal shape tells you a district or a town is ready to buy from Schola in either product, versus merely aware of the problem?
Calibrates the watch terms. The RFP for a program-management platform is one shape (Assembly); a district enrollment-decline report or a redistricting vote is another (ScholaOne). Your read is more reliable than ours here.
Weekly PDF plus CSV is our default delivery. Does that work for how Schola's team consumes data, or would a different shape land better?
Delivery surface matters as much as the signal for adoption. A great feed in the wrong format is invisible. Answer sets whether we ship an extract, a feed, or push into a CRM you already run.
The "AI-powered intelligence layer" language on Schola's site: is that a roadmap where MA could partner as a data source into what you are building, or is that already resourced internally?
Read A of your inbound (MA identifies districts and municipalities Schola sells into on either ScholaOne or Assembly) and Read B (MA feeds structured signal into Schola's own intelligence product) are different conversations with different commercial shapes. Worth knowing which is on the table before Phase 2.
