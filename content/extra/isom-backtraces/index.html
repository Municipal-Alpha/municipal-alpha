Before you lean on any of it

What these chains do and do not license

Every line here is a bound on a claim above it, said next to the claim rather than buried.

Agendas, mostly. Where a recorded vote exists I quoted it. Where it does not, the row says what staff recommended and means it. I pulled minutes on Vista specifically because an outcome was claimed. Doing it systematically is a fetch away, not a capability gap
Thirty-two agendas failed to fetch. Twenty-nine at Cajon Valley and three at Norwalk-La Mirada, across the four districts I read back to 2019. If a chain above looks like it has a gap, that gap could be the record or it could be my fetch, and those thirty-two are where I would look first
Attachments and images. The Vista chain reads agendas and attached minutes, not every attachment behind every item. Two of the most important Cajon Valley documents, a seventeen page bond workshop deck and a twenty two page Dale Scott presentation, are image only. I can tell you those documents exist, on those dates, attached to those items. Nothing on this page quotes them
Paramount's arc is historical. I chose it partly because it is checkable end to end, which is also its limitation. It proves the model held once, in one district. It is not evidence about the other 599
Lead time is not a promise, and not a claim of exclusivity. 1,029 days is what Vista's record shows for this bond. Vista's prior cycle ran 742 days from plan to resolution and the failed 2024 attempt ran 357. One district is not a distribution. All of these agendas are published openly, so the claim is about aggregation and attention, not about being the only one who could have seen it
