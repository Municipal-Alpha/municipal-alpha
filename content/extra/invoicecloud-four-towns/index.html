InvoiceCloud · Tue 2026-10-06

Four towns, read from their own records

Two InvoiceCloud accounts drawn at random, Manchester, NH and Hagerstown, MD, are both in the middle of a core-system change. The public record shows Manchester consolidating payments onto Tyler and Hagerstown keeping InvoiceCloud. Westbrook, ME and Hattiesburg, MS, two towns that are not your accounts, are here for comparison. Every row was read from the towns' own records and websites as they stood on 2 October 2026, and every item below links to its source. Three things back from you would move this forward: what your Manchester CSM makes of the first item, your own list of invoice types, and which first block of accounts you want.

What's worth knowing right now

The leading edge of the event stream: things on the record that will change a future snapshot but have not changed it yet.

  • Manchester, NH is moving card payments onto Tyler Payments, one department at a time, and the departments it named next are the ones on InvoiceCloud.

    Water and sewer billing came first, the city's payment page named Paymentus until 18 September, and the Tyler agreement runs from 1 October. At the 7 July Committee on Accounts the Finance Manager said "eventually the remainder of the city, we're going to bring the rest of them on to that one platform," and the Finance Officer pointed to the Clerk's office and Tax as where "this could be another way around, just using one processor." Both took payments through InvoiceCloud when we read them on 2 October.

    Sources: Committee on Accounts minutes, 7 Jul 2026 · Board minutes, 7 Jul 2026 · Water Works page, read 30 Sep 2026

  • Hagerstown, MD is moving to Workday plus SpryPoint, and the SpryPoint work keeps InvoiceCloud in place.

    The SpryPoint and Workday subscription fees were on the council's December 2025 agenda. The signed SpryPoint statement of work in the July 2026 packet specifies a two-way integration between SpryCIS and InvoiceCloud, covering account lookups, statements, payment postback and autopay.

    Sources: Council agenda packet, 16 Dec 2025 · Council agenda packet, 28 Jul 2026

  • Westbrook, ME voted to move recreation payments to CivicPlus.

    The council added CivicPlus for recreation registration payments, replacing what the packet calls "two separate vendors" with variable fees, at a flat 3% that the city absorbs. The city's recreation page still linked to Rec1 when we read it on 30 September, which is why the snapshot row below says Rec1. That gap is the difference between the snapshot and the stream.

    Sources: Council agenda packet, 8 Sep 2025 · Council minutes, 8 Sep 2025

  • Hattiesburg, MS is adopting Tyler Payments across "all applicable" departments.

    The quotation marks are the budget presentation's own words. It does not say which departments those are.

    Source: FY2027 budget presentation, 21 Jul 2026

What the next version changes

  1. The same list in every town. Every town gets the same invoice types and the same roles, so a missing row is a finding rather than a gap in our reading. Where a town only takes something at the counter, the row says so. Stephen asked about building permits in Manchester: they are in the table, third from the bottom, as in_person.
  2. An IT contact in every power map, even when it is one person or an outside firm. Gary put it plainly: in a lot of these towns "it's going to just say IT guy."
  3. Revenue by invoice type. Each invoice type gets the town's own budgeted revenue for it and its trend across the budget years we hold, so the white space above can be sorted by dollars.

Depth per town stays fixed. What changes with scope is how many towns.

How to read the tables

Each town has two tables. The snapshot is what is live and true on the day it is taken, meaning every way the town bills, who processes it and on what terms, and the people who decide. The event stream is what the town is saying in agendas, minutes and budgets that will change a later snapshot. The snapshot would run monthly; events arrive as the records do, and could land straight in your data warehouse.

highTwo or more independent sources agree, and at least one is a board record, a contract or a live web page we read and dated. No source disagrees. mediumOne such source. lowOnly indirect signs, such as a vendor's customer list or traces in a website's code. conflictSources disagree. Both are kept and flagged for a person to check. Manchester's water row is one: the old processor's page and the new agreement were both current in the same week.

Two pages on the same website count as one source, and a row whose evidence is more than 12 months old drops one level. The words in the cells mean:

in_personNo online way to pay when we read it; the town takes it at the counter. not_publishedLooked for and not found anywhere the town publishes. Contract end dates usually land here, since towns post agendas and minutes, not signed agreements, and with no contract in hand the term is unknown. A records request can get the agreement. not_statedThe source covers the item but doesn't say. nullThe fact exists and we don't have it yet. N/AThe column doesn't apply, for example contract terms on an in-person row.
