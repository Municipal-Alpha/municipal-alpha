Three Kentucky towns the data center story did not name

On August 17 Kentucky Public Radio reported bipartisan outrage over hyperscale data centers, naming Mercer County, Burgin, Louisville and Oldham County. The same fight was already in the public record of three other Kentucky towns, the earliest in June. Every line below links to the document it came from.

John, this is the version for the seven-station collaborative. Their beat this month is data centers, so the test is whether the municipal record adds towns, dates or documents to a story they are already reporting well. Part one is that. Part two is a second subject, Flock cameras, read the same way. Part three is what we do not hold, which across three states is a lot, and it is named rather than hidden.

Part one · one subject, read across towns

Data center moratoria in Oak Grove, Georgetown and Shelbyville

Agendas record what a body scheduled, not what it decided. Where a line comes from an agenda the verb is "scheduled" or "on the agenda." Where it comes from minutes, the record is quoted.

Jun 2, 2026Oak Grove, KY · council minutes

Under old business, in a discussion about RV parks, the record turns to data centers and to the neighboring city.

Council Member Edward Cook also stated that the City of Hopkinsville is developing language regarding AI data centers and will share that information with Oak Grove for consideration in determining how the city wishes to proceed.

Two days later the committee of the whole took it up again: "Committee Member Isaiah Spencer inquired about AI data centers. Committee Members Leavell and Cook reported that the City of Hopkinsville is currently developing language related to this topic. The committee will review the proposed language once Tom Britton with CDS completes the draft."

Jun 22, 2026Georgetown, KY · council agenda

The last item of council discussion on the June 22 agenda:

New Business – Discussion – Data Center Moratorium

An agenda line, nothing more, and that is what it is presented as. It puts the word "moratorium" in Georgetown's record eight weeks before the August 17 story.

Jul 16, 2026Shelbyville, KY · council minutes

The council reaches a consensus and sets a date.

There was a discussion on Data Centers between council and the mayor. A workshop will be in place to discuss further activities. It was a consensus of the council members to recommend that a moratorium on future data centers be established for the City of Shelbyville with a first reading to be placed on the agenda at the council meeting on August 6, 2026.

Then, under public comments: "Denise Vandevelde is concerned that a gas pipeline going through her yard and the pipeline will be given to data centers." A named resident, a pipeline, a data center, in the minutes, a month before the story.

Three towns, none of them in the story, on the record between 32 and 76 days before it ran. Shelbyville is a five-week arc with a resident's name and a pipeline in it. Oak Grove's minutes point at a fourth town, Hopkinsville, drafting the language. None of this is a correction of the reporting. It is the next three phone calls.

Part three · what we did not see

What it refuses to do, and where the record is thin

It does not infer. If a document says a reading is scheduled, the page says scheduled. It does not say a moratorium passed unless minutes record the vote. It does not quote a document it could not read. And a town that is absent here is absent because we do not read it yet, not because it was quiet.
