Why this looks different

This edition is built on what three of you said about the last ones, not timely, not newsworthy, mostly already known, and on what Scott asked for on Friday: lean harder into the read, point at what else the record could support, and line the brief up with the six reader needs the newsroom sorts stories by.

What changed, in the order you will see it. A thread you have seen before says so in its first line, and only the documents added since the last edition are marked NEW. An item that is only Update Me, the kind you were in the room for, is listed in one line at the end rather than written up again. Portland City Council weighs Public Art Committee's FY27 Budget plan

If you care about public art in Portland, the city council was scheduled to consider the Public Art Committee's annual report and budget plan on August 24, 2026. Serves: Update me.

What the record says, angles, impact, 3 documents

What the record says

The Portland City Council was scheduled to consider accepting the Portland Public Art Committee Fiscal Year 2026 Annual Report and Fiscal Year 2027 Budget Plan on August 24, 2026. The Public Art Committee, chaired by Kat Zagaria Buckley, sponsored this order. The Portland Public Art Committee voted 8-0 on July 15, 2026, to recommend the FY26 Annual Report and FY27 Budget Plan to the Portland City Council.

Angles the record supports

Help me: The City Council was scheduled to consider approving administrative fees for services such as Fire Department outside detail fees, Parking Division garage fees, and Parks, Recreation & Facilities fees. (1 June agenda)

Give me perspective: The Portland Downtown District, established in 1992, is funded by special assessments on real estate, with an estimated mil rate (the property tax rate, dollars per $1,000 of assessed value) of $1.07 per $1,000 of property value for FY2027. (15 June agenda)
