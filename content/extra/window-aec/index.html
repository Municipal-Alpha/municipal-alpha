The project exists before the RFP does.

By the time a solicitation reaches the bid channels, the shortlist conversations have often already happened. The project lived in board minutes for months first — the workshop, the study committee, the capital-plan line, the motion to engage a consultant. Firms that read the record show up before the field forms.

The pattern, from public records
Month 0
A board names a problem — a failing culvert, a water study, a building past its life. It is an agenda item, not a project.
Months 2–6
A committee forms. Engineering opinions are requested. The scope takes shape in minutes and packets, town by town.
Month 8+
If an RFP appears at all, it reflects conversations that already happened. In many towns it never does — the work is awarded from the process above.

The Amherst NH tower case ran 23 months from first mention to award — with no RFP ever issued.
