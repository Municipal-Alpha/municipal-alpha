What kind of documents they are, which is the honest part

The breakdown matters more than the total, because most of it is not what a demo would want you to think it is.

83 check registers
A town paying its InvoiceCloud invoice. That is an accounts-payable line, not a contract event. Useful as a payment and continuity signal, useless as a procurement signal, and anyone who sells you these as “contract activity” is selling you a check register.

The deliberative layer. An agenda states what a body intends to do; minutes record what it did. These are the rows where a renewal or an award actually appears.

The layer underneath: what towns actually pay, to whom

Accounts payable is the largest structured thing we hold. 15,440 check registers across 132 municipalities in 36 states. Every vendor a town paid, and how much.

For a payments company that is two different products depending on which question you are asking, and I do not know which one you are here for:

Where is a contract coming up
The deliberative layer, agendas and minutes, read for renewal and award language. This is the pre-decision window and it is the hardest of the three.
Who holds it today
The AP layer, read as an incumbency census. Which town pays which processor, at what cadence, and when a payment stream to one vendor stops and another begins.
What is inside the terms
Fee schedules, convenience-fee ordinances, and the finance-committee arguments around them. Partially reachable, and the least even of the three.

What I will not claim

The number that matters most on this page is the one that limits every other number on it.

Coverage denominator
Counts here describe the searchable slice of what we hold, not the United States. Nationally about a quarter of collected documents are full-text searchable today. Every figure above is a floor, and I will always give you the denominator next to the count.
Absence is not evidence
A town where I find no processor is a town where I have not found one. It is not a town without one, and I will never hand you a prospect list built on that confusion.
Homonyms are the real work
In an adjacent search this month, “Spectrum” resolved to a sign company and “Grande” to a legal-aid nonprofit. A keyword count that has not been read is a guess with a number attached to it.
Verb licence
An agenda item reading “Approving an Application for...” is a proposal, not an approval. Municipal agendas state the intended disposition in the title, so every agenda-sourced signal reads like a completed decision unless something stops it. We gate that.
