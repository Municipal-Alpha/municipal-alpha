What closes a blank

The question is not how many gaps that table has, it is what each costs to fill. Three answers, very different prices.

1 · We have it, unprocessed
The answer sits in a document already in the corpus, in a scan with no text layer or history not yet extracted. Grafton's clock and Lisbon's ERP are both this. I want to be exact about what “we have it” means, because the loose version of this claim is the kind a buyer should not accept. We hold a catalogue of 962,230 municipal documents, each with its source URL, board, meeting date and type. We hold the text of 203,129 of them. Between those sits a reachable backlog of 642,448 documents that are catalogued and fetchable but unread, which is a queue rather than a gap. On a 300 document sample, fetching and reading that backlog returned usable text for 59% and a scan needing OCR for another 29%. Your list sets the order it gets worked.
2 · It has not happened yet
Nothing is missing, the town has not decided. Derby signs in June, Pocatello just terminated its financial system. Time closes these, which is what a recurring monitor is for. Towns in an engagement stay on the daily crawl, so a new posting is in the corpus within a day of appearing; reading it is a queue and the honest commitment is a working week, not an hour. The monitor is priced after the first delivery, not folded into it.
3 · Never published
Some agreements are executed and never posted. Those we go and get as public records. Slower, and not every one comes back. It is not in the block price and I am not going to bundle it as a promise, because the timeline on a records request belongs to the town rather than to me.
