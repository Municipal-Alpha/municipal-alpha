Morgantown, the week six boards met and one had a reporter in the room

Black By God's folk reporter covered the August 11 Morgantown City Council meeting. Five other city boards met the same week, and the council packet carried the minutes of the meeting before. This is what was in the record that nobody had to attend to read, every line linked to the document it came from.

John, this is the Black By God version of the page I sent you on August 11. I picked Morgantown because the Civics page already runs "What You Missed at Morgantown City Council," so the test is simple: does the record hold anything the person in the room did not get. Nothing here has been sent to the newsroom.

Part one is the week. Part two follows one subject, Flock cameras, from a Morgantown council remark in July to the public record in four Kentucky cities. Part three is what we could not read, which for this audience is the part that matters.

Part two · one subject, read across towns

Flock cameras, from a council remark in Morgantown to four Kentucky cities

Black By God's Civics page leads with Huntington's Flock surveillance contract. The same subject is in the Morgantown record, and it is in the public record of four Kentucky cities this spring, each at a different stage. Read together, they show what a city says when residents ask, what it budgets, and what it says once the cameras are up.

May 11, 2026Newport, KY · commission minutes

The commission voted 5-0 to take public comment on a police technology presentation. The minutes record the city manager's answer to residents before the demonstration.

City Manager Hayden addressed public concerns regarding the proposed Flock Camera system. He explained that the cameras capture only license plate information, do not use facial recognition, and will not be used for traffic enforcement. He stated that the system has been tested through a free pilot program, all searches are audited, and the City will not share data with federal immigration authorities unless required by court order.

Then a resident, Max Long, asked whether police would need a plate number to use it. The chief said no: "the system can identify and search for vehicles using characteristics such as make, model, color, and other distinguishing features. The cameras create a 'fingerprint' of each vehicle."

Part three · what we did not see

What it refuses to do, and where the record is thin

It does not infer. If a document says a hearing is scheduled, the brief says scheduled. It does not say a vote happened unless minutes record the vote. It does not quote a document it could not read. And it does not claim a town is quiet when the truth is that we could not reach the town's server.

Coverage, as of August 21, 2026 · the part that can go red
Morgantown · 620 documents held · latest meeting 2026-08-20
The city's server refuses requests from datacenter addresses, so its August agendas arrived as corrupted downloads. Fourteen of eighteen were recovered today over a residential connection; four are scanned images awaiting OCR. Everything quoted above was read in full after recovery. This is a known class of failure with a standing fix, not a gap in what the city publishes.
