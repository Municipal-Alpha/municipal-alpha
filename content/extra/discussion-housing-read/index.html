The answer, partly

Cumberland reads its own permit census into the minutes, unprompted

Approved projects under construction: Oceanview phase II · 33/52 permits have been issued; Orchard Road · Cortland Court · 8 of 10 building permits issued as of this date; Snowy Owl off Old Gray Road · 10 units approved … Mineral Springs off Blackstrap Rd · 26/72 permits issued Cumberland Lands & Conservation Commission minutes, 4 March 2026

Approvals against permits actually pulled, project by project, in a town's own minutes. That is the LD 184 numerator and denominator, published voluntarily, eight hours of manila folders already done. Cumberland is one of Paul's towns, and it is one town in one month, so treat it as an existence proof rather than a regional dataset. The question worth answering this morning is how many of the 64 do this, and how consistently, because that number decides whether a machine read of the minutes beats the survey's response rate or merely supplements it.

What the minutes will not give you is the subsidy question directly. Very few boards write "low-income housing tax credit" into a decision. My read is that you reach it by elimination, cross-referencing approvals against the MaineHousing and LIHTC award lists so the non-subsidised projects surface without anyone disclosing anything. That is a method, not a finding, and I would rather test it against your judgement than build it and hope.

Where the paper actually is

One town carries the housing record in each region

Same measure, same twelve months, both service areas. The useful part is where the towns diverge.

Greater Portland · GPCOG

Falmouth, 251 of 504

Half the region's entitlement paper sits in one town, on a healthy denominator of 870 readable documents. Then Portland 71, Yarmouth 47, Cumberland 27, Gorham 21.

Southern Maine · SMPDC

Saco, 188 of 293

Nearly two thirds, on 831 readable documents. Then Biddeford 43, Acton 18, Eliot 15, South Berwick 12.

Quiet, and provably so

Four hits each

Kennebunk and York, across 223 and 136 readable documents. On denominators that size the quiet is real rather than our blindness.

Falmouth and Saco are the same finding twice, in two agencies that do not otherwise compare notes. Whether that is a genuine concentration of housing activity or simply the two best record-keepers in southern Maine is a question your members can answer and my corpus cannot. Either answer is worth having: one is a housing signal, the other is a coverage map of your own membership.

One more, because it is on the calibration sheet

Gorham approved a subdivision and adopted a moratorium inside 24 hours

ITEM 1 Public Hearing · Final Subdivision and Site Plan Review · Troiano Properties LLC · Gorham Fairways South · A request for final review of a plan for phases 1 and 2 for a mixed-use development Gorham Planning Board minutes, 1 June 2026

The following day the Town Council adopted a moratorium on "the issuance of any development permit, license, or other regulatory approval … related to any new or expanded Data Center." Moratorium is James's own calibration keyword, Gorham is one of Paul's towns, and the subject is data centers rather than housing. That is the interesting part: the reflex arrives in a town before the subject does, and it is watchable across all 64 boards from the same measure.

Said before you ask

What this read cannot tell you

Kittery
Reads zero, and that is my gap rather than Kittery's. I hold over 400 of its documents for the window and can read five of them today. It is also the town James named first, so it is first in the queue.
Fifteen towns
Not in the corpus at all, all of them inland York and Oxford County. The read says nothing about them either way. Greater Portland's blind spots are Gray, Sebago, Casco, Raymond and the islands.
Portland
71 entitlement documents but zero carrying approval language, which almost certainly means its minutes are not filing as minutes on my side. Do not read Portland's approval column as a finding.
The measure
Counts documents rather than projects. One subdivision generates paper for months, so Falmouth's 251 is volume, not 251 projects.

The honest denominator is the product. I would rather hand you a number with its blind spots named than a cleaner one you later catch out.
