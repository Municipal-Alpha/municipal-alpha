The periodic table of municipal elements

Every kind of record a town, county or district produces is an element. Some are abundant, some are rare, and some we know exist but have not collected yet. On their own they are raw material. Combined, they answer questions no single document can.

How the table grows

The table is not fixed. In May, a row of elements was theoretical: investment policies, special districts, county records, vendor contracts, insurance renewals, historic district records. Each one has since turned up in the record. Looking for one element tends to surface its neighbours, so every hunt adds to the next edition. Elements move one way: theoretical, then found, then read, then structured.

Compounds

The useful answers are rarely in one element. They form when elements combine across a town's records, over time.

  • Mn + Ld + Cr

    Decision to payment

    A vote in the minutes, followed to the check that paid for it.

    Minutes · Legislative record · Check register

  • Rf + Bd + Mn + Cr

    Who holds the contract

    The solicitation, the bids, the award and the payments that follow it.

    Request for proposals · Bid results · Minutes · Check register

  • Bg + Rf + Jp + Mn

    A system change in motion

    A town replacing a core system shows up in its budget, its bids, its hiring and its minutes before it goes live.

    Budget · Request for proposals · Job description & posting · Minutes

  • Mp + Sb + Ci + Pr

    Where growth is headed

    The plan, the plats, the capital spend and the permits that make it real.

    Master plan · Subdivision & site plan · Capital plan · Permit record

  • Au + Pn + Ds + Tr

    Fiscal strain

    Audit findings, retiree obligations, debt due and the tax rate needed to cover them.

    Audit · Pension & retiree health · Debt service schedule · Tax rate recap

  • Bg + Fe + Ur + Tb

    What a town collects, and from what

    Revenue by source: the budget line, the fee behind it, the rate study and the cash that arrives.

    Budget · Fee schedule · Utility rate study · Treasurer's report

Isotopes and allotropes

Isotopes: one element, many forms

A check register from one town's finance system looks nothing like the next town's. Same element, different layout. Treating them as one element is what lets a payment in one town be compared with a payment in another.

Allotropes: one substance, different structures

A budget appears as the budget book, as the manager's budget message, and as slides inside a meeting packet. The same material, presented three ways, and each form says something the others leave out.

Dark matter

A large share of what towns publish arrives without saying what it is: a file called "10A.pdf", a document library with no labels. We hold it, and identifying it is where new elements come from.
