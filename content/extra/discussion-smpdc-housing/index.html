Starting from what you said you were trying to do, on 25 June

The data LD 184 asks your towns to dig out is already in their minutes

You described an existing-conditions analysis of the region's affordable housing stock, and the specific thing the state survey does not reach. This is a first pass at whether the public record can get you there, run across your 39 towns and Greater Portland's 25 with the same measure.

Your framing, not mine

What you said you needed

What we want to know is what is the breakdown between … those with affordable housing tax credits or other forms of subsidy versus those that are nonsubsidized, that they are affordable housing being built pursuant to density bonuses. James Rather, recorded call, 25 June 2026

And on why the survey does not answer it:

What that survey does is it requires communities over 4,000 population and up to provide survey data on CFOs, building permits issued. But one of the things it doesn't capture are some of the nuances of affordable housing that we want to better understand. For example, what is the use of subsidy in these projects? James Rather, on LD 184, 25 June 2026

And what it costs the towns that do answer it:

Some of them reported, we had to spend eight hours going through hard copy files to find the stuff you need. We had to [go] back to the public works, into the archives of project approvals, to a bunch of manila folders … It's very very rudimentary in a lot of our smaller communities, the way they catch the data. James Rather, 25 June 2026

That is the problem I went looking for in the record. Not whether housing is happening, which you already know, but whether the subtypes you cannot get from the survey are sitting in the minutes anyway.

The answer, partly

Cumberland reads its own permit census into the minutes, unprompted

Approved projects under construction: Oceanview phase II · 33/52 permits have been issued; Orchard Road · Cortland Court · 8 of 10 building permits issued as of this date; Snowy Owl off Old Gray Road · 10 units approved … Mineral Springs off Blackstrap Rd · 26/72 permits issued Cumberland Lands & Conservation Commission minutes, 4 March 2026

Approvals against permits actually pulled, project by project, in a town's own minutes. That is the LD 184 numerator and denominator, published voluntarily, eight hours of manila folders already done. One town in one month, so treat it as an existence proof rather than a regional dataset. The question worth answering on the call is how many of the 64 towns do this, and how consistently, because that number decides whether this replaces the survey or merely supplements it.

What the minutes will not give you is the subsidy question directly. Very few boards write "low-income housing tax credit" into a decision. My read is that you reach it by elimination, cross-referencing approvals against the MaineHousing and LIHTC award lists so the non-subsidised projects surface without anyone disclosing anything. That is a method, not a finding, and I would rather test it against your judgement than build it and hope.
