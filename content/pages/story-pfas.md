<h2 style="font-family:'Source Serif 4',serif; font-size:24px; font-weight:500; margin:0 0 14px;">The pattern</h2>
<p style="font-size:15px; color:oklch(0.75 0.01 60); margin:0 0 16px;">PFAS contamination is a public health story. But before it becomes a public health story, it's a municipal agenda story. Somewhere between the first water test and the EPA press release, a planning board discusses it, a town council votes on a study, and an ordinance committee drafts new language. All of that activity is in public documents. We read them.</p>
<p style="font-size:15px; color:oklch(0.75 0.01 60); margin:0 0 32px;">Our pipeline has detected 190+ PFAS-related events across 80+ municipalities in 22 states. The events range from initial studies and water testing to ordinances, moratoriums, compliance actions, and remediation contracts. The pattern is consistent: once one town in a region starts testing, neighboring towns follow within months.</p>

It's a national remediation wave that's still in its early stages.</p>
<h2 style="font-family:'Source Serif 4',serif; font-size:24px; font-weight:500; margin:0 0 14px;">How it moves through a region</h2>
<p style="font-size:15px; color:oklch(0.75 0.01 60); margin:0 0 8px;"><b style="color:oklch(0.93 0.01 60);">One town tests.</b> A water utility runs PFAS sampling, usually prompted by state-level requirements or federal guidance.</p>
<p style="font-size:15px; color:oklch(0.75 0.01 60); margin:0 0 8px;"><b style="color:oklch(0.93 0.01 60);">Results get discussed.</b> If levels are elevated, it hits the town council agenda. If they're not, it hits the agenda anyway because residents read about the neighboring town.</p>
<p style="font-size:15px; color:oklch(0.75 0.01 60); margin:0 0 8px;"><b style="color:oklch(0.93 0.01 60);">Neighboring towns react.</b> Adjacent municipalities begin their own testing, often within the same quarter.</p>
<p style="font-size:15px; color:oklch(0.75 0.01 60); margin:0 0 8px;"><b style="color:oklch(0.93 0.01 60);">Ordinances follow.</b> Setback requirements, discharge limits, monitoring requirements. We've detected 49 PFAS-related ordinance events.</p>
<p style="font-size:15px; color:oklch(0.75 0.01 60); margin:0 0 32px;"><b style="color:oklch(0.93 0.01 60);">Money moves.</b> Studies become contracts. Monitoring becomes remediation. Remediation becomes infrastructure. Every stage has a supply chain.</p>
<h2 style="font-family:'Source Serif 4',serif; font-size:24px; font-weight:500; margin:0 0 14px;">The leading indicator</h2>
<p style="font-size:15px; color:oklch(0.75 0.01 60); margin:0 0 16px;">The 50 PFAS ordinances we've detected are the leading indicator. An ordinance creates a legal obligation to spend. The spending follows on a 6-18 month lag, procurement, contracting, construction. By the time the remediation contract shows up in a check register, the ordinance that mandated it has been in our database for a year.</p>
<p style="font-size:15px; color:oklch(0.75 0.01 60); margin:0 0 32px;">A municipal bond analyst looking at PFAS exposure is reading EPA enforcement actions, lagging indicators. We're reading the ordinances that create the spending obligation. The signal is the same. The timing is different.</p>
<p style="font-family:'Source Serif 4',serif; font-style:italic; font-size:17px; color:oklch(0.93 0.01 60); margin:0 0 32px;">The question isn't whether PFAS remediation will be a significant infrastructure spending category. It's which municipalities are already committed, and which are next. We know. It's in their agendas.</p>
