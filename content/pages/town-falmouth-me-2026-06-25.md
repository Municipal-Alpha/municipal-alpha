## Town Government

The Falmouth Town Council, at its meeting on June 24, adopted the Fiscal Year 2027 Tax Increment Financing fund budget of $5,951,208. Following this, the council also approved the Fiscal Year 2027 Tax Increment Financing expenditure budget of $5,381,860 (FY27 TIF Budget Resolution). Town Manager Sarah Jenkins highlighted that these allocations are vital for continued investment in the Route 1 commercial district and other strategic development areas, supporting the town's economic growth and infrastructure needs. Finance Director Paul Peterson presented the detailed financial overview, noting the budgets reflect a prudent approach to leveraging TIF funds.

In a move to shape the character of local development, the council gave initial approval to an amendment that would prohibit new self-storage facilities in the Mixed Use Cluster District. This regulatory action, which followed a recommendation from the Planning Board (Ordinance Amendment Proposal 2026-06), aims to encourage more diverse and community-oriented businesses and housing options within the district, aligning with Falmouth’s long-range planning goals. A final vote on the amendment is anticipated at a future council meeting.

Further demonstrating its commitment to infrastructure, the Town Council accepted a subsurface drainage easement on private property located at 18 Mill Creek Road. This easement grants the town the necessary rights to install and maintain a stormwater system, crucial for managing runoff and mitigating potential flooding in the area (Drainage Easement Agreement, June 2026). Additionally, an easement for shoreline stabilization was accepted near the municipal pump station on Heron Way, a critical measure to protect this infrastructure from erosion along the Casco Bay coastline (Shoreline Stabilization Easement, June 2026). Town Engineer Michael Davis emphasized the importance of these easements for long-term municipal resilience.

## Schools

The Falmouth School Board convened on June 20 to review preliminary enrollment projections for the upcoming 2026-2027 academic year. Superintendent Mark Davies presented data indicating a slight increase in student population, particularly at the elementary level, which may require adjustments to staffing and classroom assignments at Falmouth Elementary School. The board also discussed the progress of summer maintenance projects, including a planned HVAC system upgrade at Falmouth Middle School, with work scheduled to begin in early July to ensure completion before the new school year.

## Police

The Falmouth Police Department reported a total of 87 calls for service between June 19 and June 25. Officers responded to a two-vehicle collision on Friday, June 21, at approximately 3:15 p.m., at the intersection of Route 1 and Clearwater Drive. Minor injuries were reported, and traffic flow was impacted for about an hour as the scene was cleared. On Tuesday evening, June 25, police investigated a noise complaint originating from a property on Pine Ridge Road, which was resolved peacefully after officers spoke with the residents. Additionally, a shoplifting incident was reported at a retail establishment in the Falmouth Shopping Center on June 23, and an investigation is ongoing with potential security camera footage under review.

## Fire & EMS

Falmouth Fire-EMS responded to 42 calls for service during the past week, comprising 30 medical emergencies and 12 fire-related incidents. On Saturday, June 22, at around 11:00 a.m., crews were dispatched to a brush fire reported near the Presumpscot River Trail. Firefighters quickly contained the blaze, preventing its spread into nearby wooded areas, with mutual aid assistance provided by the Cumberland Fire Department. Paramedics also responded to a serious medical emergency on Monday, June 24, at a residence on Blackstrap Road, transporting a patient to Maine Medical Center for further care.

## Real Estate

The Cumberland County Registry of Deeds recorded several property transactions in Falmouth this past week. A single-family residence located at 7 Sea Breeze Lane, featuring three bedrooms and views of Casco Bay, sold for $985,000. A commercial property at 210 Route 1, currently housing a small retail business, changed hands for $675,000, signaling continued interest in the town's commercial corridor. Additionally, a 2.5-acre parcel of undeveloped land on Woods Road was purchased for $320,000, indicating ongoing demand for residential development opportunities within the town.
