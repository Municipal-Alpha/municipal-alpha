Summary: Towns are rewriting their rules for backyard homes in 2026, and the reason shows up in their own public records first. In three towns that adopted new ADU rules this year, the first sign sat in their minutes 5 months to more than 3 years before the vote.

<h2 style="font-family:'Source Serif 4',serif; font-size:24px; font-weight:500; margin:0 0 14px;">The pattern</h2>
<p style="font-size:15px; color:oklch(0.75 0.01 60); margin:0 0 32px;">Accessory dwelling units, or ADUs, are the backyard cottage, the apartment over the garage and the in-law suite on a single-family lot. States are pushing towns to allow them. Towns write the rest: which lots, how big, how tall, where the car goes, and whether it can be rented by the night. Those decisions are made in planning boards and council chambers, and they are written down.</p>
<h2 style="font-family:'Source Serif 4',serif; font-size:24px; font-weight:500; margin:0 0 14px;">It started in the minutes</h2>
<p style="margin:0 0 28px; font-family:'Source Serif 4',serif; font-size:clamp(26px,4.5vw,34px); line-height:1.25; color:oklch(0.93 0.01 60);"><span style="color:oklch(0.78 0.14 75);">5 months to over 3 years</span> from the first sign in a town’s own minutes to the rule it adopted.</p>
<p style="font-size:15px; color:oklch(0.75 0.01 60); margin:0 0 16px;">The first sign is the earliest point where the problem shows up in the town’s own record: a homeowner’s wasted plans, a state law the town’s code now breaks, a worry about older neighbors.</p>

<h2 style="font-family:'Source Serif 4',serif; font-size:24px; font-weight:500; margin:0 0 14px;">Why it matters</h2>
<p style="font-size:15px; color:oklch(0.75 0.01 60); margin:0 0 32px;">Who may build an ADU, how big it can be and whether it can be rented are decided town by town. For anyone who builds, sells, finances or insures backyard homes, the next rule is already taking shape in a town’s minutes, often months before the vote.
