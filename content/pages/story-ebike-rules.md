Summary: Towns are tightening e-bike rules in 2026, and the warning shows up in their own public records first. In three towns that adopted rules this year, the first warning sat in their minutes 3 to 14 months before the vote.

<h2 style="font-family:'Source Serif 4',serif; font-size:24px; font-weight:500; margin:0 0 14px;">The pattern</h2>
<p style="font-size:15px; color:oklch(0.75 0.01 60); margin:0 0 32px;">E-bikes and e-scooters spread faster than the rules for them. States set the basic classes. Towns decide the rest: who may ride, where, how fast, and with what on their heads. Those decisions are made in council chambers and select board meetings, and they are written down.</p>
<h2 style="font-family:'Source Serif 4',serif; font-size:24px; font-weight:500; margin:0 0 14px;">The warning came first</h2>
<p style="margin:0 0 28px; font-family:'Source Serif 4',serif; font-size:clamp(26px,4.5vw,34px); line-height:1.25; color:oklch(0.93 0.01 60);"><span style="color:oklch(0.78 0.14 75);">3 to 14 months</span> from the first warning in a town’s own minutes to the rule it adopted.</p>

<h2 style="font-family:'Source Serif 4',serif; font-size:24px; font-weight:500; margin:0 0 14px;">Why it matters</h2>
<p style="font-size:15px; color:oklch(0.75 0.01 60); margin:0 0 32px;">Where an e-bike may be ridden, and by whom, is decided town by town. For anyone who sells, rents, insures or shares e-bikes and scooters, the next rule is already taking shape in a town’s minutes, usually months before the vote.
